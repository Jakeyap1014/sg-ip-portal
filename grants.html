Critical: IDI (IP Development Incentive) — CLOSED to new applicants

The IDI — Singapore's flagship 5–10% concessionary tax rate on IP income — was the primary reason to set up an SG IP Co. However, the official EDB circular confirms: "No IDI award may be approved after 31 December 2023." New applications are no longer accepted. The EIS (below) remains the most valuable accessible incentive. Engaging EDB directly to confirm whether any successor scheme exists is recommended.

Grants Removed — Not Applicable for Lifely

Why these were excluded

Each grant was assessed against Lifely's actual profile: established AU-based group, IP is primarily furniture trademarks and designs, no Singapore R&D team, SG IP Co is a new holding entity licensing to its own subsidiaries.

IP Development Incentive (IDI)
Officially closed. EDB circular confirms: "No IDI award may be approved after 31 December 2023." This was the flagship 5–10% concessionary tax rate on IP income. No longer available for new applicants. Confirm directly with EDB whether a successor scheme exists.
Startup SG Tech (POC/POV)
Designed for early-stage tech startups commercialising proprietary technology that has not yet been commercialised. Lifely's core IP (furniture trademarks, product designs) is already fully commercialised. SG IP Co is a holding entity, not a tech startup conducting R&D.
Market Readiness Assistance (MRA)
For Singapore companies entering new overseas markets to sell to external (third-party) customers. SG IP Co licenses IP to its own subsidiaries — intra-group transactions are excluded. MRA would apply to the operating entities, not the IP holding company.
RISC (Research & Innovation Scheme for Companies)
Co-funds salaries of R&D staff physically based in Singapore. Lifely has no Singapore-based R&D team. Routine IP management and licensing administration do not qualify — must be genuine science/technology innovation creating new products or processes.
EDG Co-Innovation Programme (CIP)
Requires a formal, structured cross-border R&D collaboration between the SG entity and a foreign company or institution. No such collaboration exists between Lifely SG IP Co and the AU/UK/CA Ops Cos at this stage.
IAF-ICP (A*STAR)
Deep tech research partnerships with A*STAR, NUS, or NTU. Designed for genuine scientific research in advanced fields. Furniture brand trademarks and product designs do not meet the research depth required. No existing A*STAR relationship.
Refundable Investment Credit (RIC)
Requires a sizeable, negotiated investment commitment with EDB — typically reserved for companies making hundreds of millions in Singapore capex. Not applicable at Lifely's current stage and entity size.

Next Steps
1

Incorporate SG IP Co via ACRA BizFile+

Register as Pte. Ltd. Ensure ≥ 30% local shareholding, appoint SG-resident director, and confirm the entity has genuine operational substance (not a pure brass-plate holding company).

2

File IP with IPOS — immediately triggers EIS deductions

File trademarks, patents, and designs as soon as the entity is incorporated. EIS 400% deduction accrues from the date of expenditure — every week of delay costs money.

3

Submit EDG on Business Grants Portal

Apply for EDG to subsidise IP strategy consulting and licensing structure work (50%). Must apply before the project commences — cannot be backdated.

4

Engage transfer pricing specialist

Royalty rates between SG IP Co and AU/US/UK/CA/NZ Ops Cos must be set at arm's-length rates with contemporaneous documentation. This must be done before any intercompany licence agreement is signed.

5

Engage EDB directly — confirm IDI successor

The IDI was the primary tax incentive for SG IP Cos and is now closed to new awards. Contact EDB at invest.edb.gov.sg to determine if a replacement scheme has been announced or is in development.

6

Engage AU tax specialist — CFC rules

Australia's Controlled Foreign Company rules may tax SG IP income back in Australia. Cross-border tax advice is essential before any royalty flows between the SG entity and AU Ops Co.
